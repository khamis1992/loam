# Creative product design: from audit to North-Star vision

This skill sits on top of an audit skill such as `pro-ui-ux-journey`. The audit finds what is broken. This skill decides what the product should become and makes people want it. Every rule here is concrete. If a rule cannot be checked on a mockup, it does not belong in the proposal.

## 0. Operating stance
- **Diverge, then converge.** Generate many ideas (≥30 raw) before choosing anything, then cut hard to the 8–15 that win on impact × feasibility × brand fit.
- **Brand constraints are creative fuel, not limits.** A fixed palette, a mascot and a pattern force distinctiveness. Never "fix" the brand. Express it better.
- **Show, don't describe.** Every major idea gets a high-fidelity mockup in real product context (real screen frame, real copy, real data shapes). A text-only idea counts as half an idea.
- **Build on prior work, never repeat it.** Before ideating, list every idea already proposed or shipped (roadmaps, earlier proposals, icon sets) in a "do not repeat" table. Extend those ideas, compose them into systems, or supersede them explicitly.
- **Separate facts, assumptions and goals.** Measured data is cited. Example data is labelled "illustrative". Targets are labelled "goal".

## 1. Research & benchmark teardown method
For each benchmark app, capture **pattern → why it works → how we adapt it in our brand → risk**. Steal mechanisms, not looks.

| App | Patterns worth stealing | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Duolingo | Streak with a single minimum daily action; auto-applied **streak freeze** (capped, about 2); "earn back" after a break; milestone rewards (30/100/365); mascot reacts emotionally to progress; evening "streak saver" reminder | Decoupling the streak from the daily goal lifted D14 retention by 3.3% (Duolingo blog). Low barrier plus loss aversion, with bounded forgiveness |
| Headspace | Illustration-led warmth; onboarding asks intent first ("what brings you here?"); calm empty states; content-led home that changes by time of day | Emotional safety, and personalization that is visible on day 1 |
| Airbnb | Large imagery, generous whitespace, one primary CTA per view; "price breakdown before you pay"; wishlists as low-commitment saves; category chips with illustrated icons | Trust through transparency, and browsing as play |
| Revolut | Dense yet clear dashboards; transaction feed with merchant logos and categories; instant feedback on money movement; spend analytics with clear charts | Control and clarity over money |
| Noom | Psychology-led onboarding quiz with a "your plan is ready" reveal; colour-coded foods (density, not "bad"); small daily lessons; coach persona | Commitment and consistency, and the IKEA effect of building your own plan |
| MyFitnessPal | Fast logging; macro breakdown with remaining-for-the-day framing; barcode scan; meal history reuse | Reduces effort, and "remaining" reads friendlier than "consumed" |
| Yazio | Fasting timer ring; clean macro bars; weekly summaries; recipe cards with nutrition chips | Single glanceable status |
| Calo | Weekly menu planner with swaps before cut-off; delivery tracking; macros per meal; plan pause | Core loop of subscription meal plans in the Gulf |
| Talabat / Deliveroo | Live order tracking with stage timeline, ETA and rider map; reorder; promos rail; restaurant ratings & time chips | Reduces wait anxiety and speeds repeat purchase |
| Apple Fitness | Closing-ring metaphor; trend arrows; awards with collectible badges; weekly summary; celebratory but brief animation | Visual completion drive. Apple restricts real Activity rings to Move/Exercise/Stand, so design an own-brand progress shape instead of copying rings |

Teardown output per app: 3 screenshots or sketches, 3 stolen patterns, 1 anti-pattern to avoid (dark patterns, guilt notifications, pay-to-keep-streak).

## 2. Creative ideation toolkit (use at least three per project)
- **How Might We (HMW):** Reframe each top friction as an HMW ("HMW make the next meal feel like a gift?"). Aim for ≥15 HMWs, cluster them, then vote.
- **Crazy 8s:** Eight sketches in eight minutes per key screen (home, meal detail, checkout). Force at least two "absurd" variants, because they often hold the seed of the best idea.
- **SCAMPER on each core screen:** Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify/Magnify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse. For example, Reverse becomes "the app tells you what you'll eat instead of you choosing".
- **Cross-industry analogies:** airline boarding pass (order ticket), banking statement (credit wallet), trading card (meal collection), weather app (daily nutrition forecast), music "Wrapped" (monthly recap), game world map (journey progress).
- **Worst possible idea:** Design the worst version, then invert it.
- **Constraint flips:** "Only the mascot may speak", "no numbers on screen", "one thumb, one tap per day".
- **Jobs & emotions:** For each job, write the emotion to create (relief, pride, anticipation, belonging) and design the moment that delivers it.
- **Convergence:** Score every idea 1–5 on User value, Business value, Brand distinctiveness, Feasibility and Evidence. Keep ideas with a total ≥18/25 or a "signature" flag.

## 3. North-Star vision + concept prototype
1. **Vision statement:** one sentence of the form "For <who>, <product> is the <metaphor> that <outcome>".
2. **3–5 experience principles:** each one testable ("One clear next step on every screen", "Celebrate progress, never shame").
3. **North-Star journey:** a story of one persona over 30 days, showing the 6–8 key moments (first open, first plan reveal, first delivery, first streak milestone, first swap, renewal).
4. **Vision board:** one image with the hero screens, mascot poses, motion cues, palette tokens and type specimen. It sets the mood before the details.
5. **Concept prototype:** 3–5 linked hi-fi hero flows (not every screen). Choose the flows that carry the business: first-time to paid, the daily loop, and the delivery moment.
6. **Bridge to today:** for each vision element give phase 1 (ship now), phase 2 and phase 3, so the vision is never "all or nothing".

## 4. Visual hierarchy & layout
- **Mobile grid:** a 4-column grid with 16px outer margins and 8–12px gutters. All spacing comes from the 4/8 scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64).
- **One focal point per screen.** Squint test: the primary CTA and the primary number must survive blur. Use size, then weight, then colour, then position, in that order.
- **Z/F scan in LTR, mirrored in RTL.** In Arabic the eye starts top-right: the title and key status go right, secondary actions left.
- **Thumb zone:** primary actions sit in the bottom 1/3. Use sticky bottom CTA bars for conversion screens. Destructive actions never go in the easiest reach.
- **Card rhythm:** keep consistent radii (for example 12/16/24 by component size) and consistent internal padding (16, or 20 for hero cards). Prefer whitespace to dividers.
- **Content density by intent.** Browse screens are image-led and airy; status screens are dense but scannable; decision screens (checkout, plans) show only decision data.
- **Image direction:** real food photography, same angle family (45° or top-down), consistent light, background tints from the brand palette. Never use placeholder or mismatched stock images.

## 5. Typography (Arabic + Latin, Cairo)
- **Cairo** is a dual-script Kufi-based family (Arabic plus a Titillium-based Latin), so one family keeps bilingual screens coherent. If you need a second Latin face for numbers or data, test optical x-height side by side; Inter is a common pairing.
- **Arabic sizing:** Arabic reads smaller at the same px. Set Arabic about 1.05–1.1× the Latin size, or verify visually.
- **Line height:** 1.6–1.8 unitless for Arabic body, 1.25–1.35 for headings. Never use letter-spacing on Arabic, because tracking breaks joins. Never use synthetic italic. Emphasise with weight only.
- **Example mobile scale (px, Arabic-first):** Display 32/40 Black · H1 26/36 Bold · H2 22/32 Bold · H3 18/28 Bold · Body-L 17/28 Regular · Body 15/26 Regular · Caption 13/22 SemiBold · Micro 11/18 Bold (labels only). Use at most 4 sizes per screen.
- **Numerals:** decide once (Western 0123 or Arabic-Indic ٠١٢٣), document it, and apply it everywhere, including charts, prices and dates. Use tabular figures in tables and counters.
- **Bidi:** wrap Latin runs (brand names, codes, units) in isolates. Check that punctuation lands on the correct side, and units sit after numbers.
- **Copy voice** is part of typography: short, warm, action-led verbs, and consistent person and number (for example plural address).

## 6. Colour systems & tokens (from a fixed palette)
- **Three token tiers:**
  - **Primitive:** brand hex values plus approved tints (for example 6/10/14/18% over white) and neutral opacities of the darkest brand colour.
  - **Semantic:** `bg.canvas`, `bg.surface`, `bg.subtle`, `text.primary`, `text.secondary`, `text.onAccent`, `border.subtle`, `action.primary`, `action.secondary`, `status.success`, `status.warning`, `status.danger`, `status.info`, `data.protein`, `data.carb`, `data.fat`, `data.kcal`, `category.*`.
  - **Component:** `button.primary.bg`, `chip.selected.bg`, and so on. Components reference semantic tokens only, never hex values.
- **One meaning per colour.** Map every brand colour to roles before designing, and keep the mapping identical in every screen, chart and illustration.
- **Contrast:** check with the WCAG formula, never by eye.
  - Text needs ≥4.5:1, or ≥3:1 for large text (≥18.66px bold or 24px) and UI glyphs.
  - Mid-luminance brand greens and blues often fail with white text, so use the darkest brand colour as text on them and record the computed ratio.
  - Secondary text in neutral opacity must still pass (≥4.5:1 for body).
- **Colour is never the only signal.** Pair it with an icon, label or pattern (status dots plus text, chart series plus legends and direct labels).
- **Tints carry atmosphere.** Use light tints for section backgrounds and chips, and the full colour for small high-signal elements (active states, progress fills, key numbers).
- **Palette audit:** before delivering, sample every pixel colour in the mockups and compare with the allowed list. Any off-palette value fails.

## 7. Design systems: atomic, variants, states
- **Atoms:** colour, type, space, radius, elevation, icon and motion tokens. **Molecules:** button, chip, input, toggle, badge, avatar, macro pill, price tag. **Organisms:** meal card, plan card, order timeline, wallet row, bottom sheet, nav bar, mascot moment. **Templates:** list, detail, flow step, dashboard, celebration.
- **Every component spec covers:**
  - anatomy, sizes (S/M/L), variants (primary/secondary/tertiary/destructive), RTL mirroring rules, content rules (max characters) and an accessibility note (role, label, target ≥44×44pt);
  - all states: default, pressed, focused, disabled, loading, selected, error, empty, skeleton.
- **Elevation:** at most 3 levels. Level 0 is flat. Level 1 is cards (soft, low-opacity shadow tinted by the darkest brand colour). Level 2 is sheets and floating CTAs. Express depth with shadow plus surface tint, never with dark surfaces in a light UI.
- **Naming:** use `category/component/variant/state`, and keep a changelog and a "deprecated" list (for example legacy icon sources).
- **Coverage map:** show a matrix of screens × components to prove consistency, and kill one-off components.

## 8. Illustration & mascot-led storytelling
- **Mascot bible:** approved construction, proportions, line weight, palette, and a "never" list. Every new pose must pass that list. Do not "improve" the character.
- **Pose library by emotion and job:** welcome, pointing (guidance), thinking (loading), celebrating (milestone, without forbidden effects), sleeping or waiting (empty), concerned but kind (error), delivering (order), and a reading or teaching pose for tips.
- **Mascot rules of use:**
  - at most one mascot per screen, and never on dense decision screens like payment;
  - the mascot points to the action and never covers it;
  - the mascot speaks in short brand voice, in 1–2 lines.
- **Narrative arc:** the mascot is the user's companion across the journey (meets them, prepares their plan, delivers, celebrates, reminds kindly). Map mascot moments onto the journey map.
- **Spot illustrations** share the mascot's stroke and palette and match the icon set's line style.

## 9. Motion design
- **Purpose first.** Every animation explains (where something came from or went), confirms (an action happened), guides (where to look next) or delights (a rare, earned moment).
- **Durations** (Material 3 token reference):
  - micro 100–150ms (press, toggle);
  - small 200–250ms (chips, small reveals);
  - medium 300–400ms (sheets, card expand);
  - large 450–600ms (full-screen transitions);
  - celebration ≤1200ms, and it must be skippable.
  - Exits are shorter than entries (for example a 400ms enter and a 200ms exit).
- **Easing** (CSS):
  - standard `cubic-bezier(0.2,0,0,1)`;
  - enter (emphasized decelerate) `cubic-bezier(0.05,0.7,0.1,1)`;
  - exit (emphasized accelerate) `cubic-bezier(0.3,0,0.8,0.15)`;
  - expressive overshoot for playful UI `cubic-bezier(0.34,1.56,0.64,1)`.
  - Springs (stiffness and damping) suit mascot and physical gestures.
- **Choreography:** stagger list items by 30–50ms (at most about 6 items, then the rest appear together). Move the hero element first, then the supporting elements. Share elements between list and detail (card to page). Keep one moving focal point at a time.
- **Formats:** Lottie (After Effects export, small JSON) for linear illustrations. Rive (state machines, interactive inputs) for a mascot that reacts to state (idle, happy, thinking, sleep). Keep file sizes small and loop idles subtly.
- **Reduced motion:** with OS reduce-motion on, replace scale, spin, parallax and big translations with cross-fades or colour changes. Stop auto-advancing carousels and loops. Never convey meaning only through motion; pair it with text or haptics.
- **Haptics:** light impact on select, success notification on completion, and never on scroll.
- **Spec format per animation:** trigger, elements, property, from→to, duration, delay, easing, reduced-motion fallback, haptic.

## 10. Micro-interactions & delight
- **Structure (Saffer):** trigger → rules → feedback → loops/modes.
- **Delight budget:** 80% of interactions are invisible and fast, 15% give subtle feedback (checkmark morph, count-up numbers, card lift), and 5% are signature moments (first plan reveal, milestone, first delivery). Rarity keeps delight special.
- **Ideas bank:** add-to-plan item that "flies" into the plan tab; macro numbers count up; swipe-to-swap a meal; pull-to-refresh with the mascot; long-press for a quick nutrition peek; shake-free undo snackbars; a progress shape that fills as meals are eaten; a sound-off-by-default chime.
- **Never** let delight delay the task. Delight must be interruptible and must not repeat on every visit.

## 11. Gamification & habit loops (ethical)
- **Hook loop:** trigger (time-of-day or delivery event) → action (one minimal step, such as confirm or rate a meal) → variable reward (mascot reaction, tip, badge) → investment (preferences, saved meals, streak). Design all four for the core daily loop.
- **Streaks:**
  - count the minimum meaningful action, not perfection;
  - auto-applied, capped freezes, plus "earn back" within 24–48h;
  - show the calendar history and milestone ladder (3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 100).
- **Progress:** weekly goal progress beats daily pressure, and the goal-gradient effect makes progress visible near completion. Show "remaining" rather than "consumed".
- **Rewards:** prefer intrinsic and status rewards (badges, collectible mascot poses, app icon variants, recap cards) over discounts. If you use credits, make the rules explicit and capped.
- **Ethics guardrails:**
  - no shaming copy, guilt-trip pushes, fake scarcity, hidden cancellation or pay-to-save-streak;
  - for nutrition and health, never praise under-eating or extreme deficits;
  - celebrate consistency and balance;
  - offer a way to pause goals without losing the history.

## 12. Personalization
- **Explicit:** goals, allergens and dislikes, schedule, delivery windows, portion size, language.
- **Implicit:** ratings, swaps, reorder patterns, time of day.
- **Visible personalization:** use labels like "picked for you because…", "fits your goal" verdicts and allergen-safe badges. Explain why to build trust, and always let users edit or override.
- **Adaptive home:** modules reorder by state (new, trial, active, paused, expiring) and time of day (morning shows the next meal; evening shows tomorrow's preview and swap window).
- **Cold start:** a 3–5 question intent quiz with a progress bar, plus sensible defaults. Never ask what you will not use visibly.

## 13. Onboarding patterns
- **Value before account:** let users browse real menus and prices as a guest. Gate only at commit (checkout).
- **Quiz → reveal:** ask intent questions, then a personalized "your plan" reveal with the mascot (a Noom/Headspace-style pattern). Show the price per meal and per day at the reveal.
- **Progressive profiling:** ask for extra details later, at the moment they are useful.
- **Permission priming:** before an OS prompt (notifications, location), show a branded explainer screen with the benefit. Ask in context.
- **Measure:** step-by-step drop-off, time to first value, quiz completion.

## 14. Empty states as marketing
- **Formula:** illustration (mascot pose) + what this place will hold + one action that fills it + optional social proof or example.
- **Types:** first use (educate and invite), user-cleared (celebrate: "all done"), no results (suggest nearest matches or relax filters), error (cause + fix + retry), offline (cached content plus a banner).
- **Skeletons** mirror the final layout, use shimmer at low contrast, and switch to a static version under reduced motion.

## 15. Data visualization for nutrition
- **Glanceable first:** one hero metric (energy remaining or progress), then macros as horizontal bars or segmented shapes, each with direct labels and a gram value.
- **Colour roles:** keep the colours consistent (protein, carb, fat, kcal) everywhere. Never encode meaning with colour alone; add a label or icon.
- **Charts:** weekly bars for consistency and trends with gentle lines. Avoid pie charts with more than 3 slices and avoid 3D. Always show the goal line, and show units.
- **Framing:** compare to the user's goal, not to an ideal body. Avoid red-for-bad food judgments, and use neutral or "balance" language.
- **Recaps:** weekly summary cards and a monthly "Wrapped"-style story (most-loved meal, streak, cuisines tried), designed as shareable portrait cards.

## 16. Conversion design for subscriptions
- **Plan comparison:** at most 3 plans, a vertical comparison on mobile, a recommended plan pre-selected with an honest reason, price per meal and per day anchored, and differences only (not repeated features).
- **Transparency:** total before pay, renewal date, and "pause, skip or cancel anytime" stated near the CTA. Show trust signals (secure payment, local support).
- **Friction removal:** Apple Pay or local wallets, saved addresses, one-screen checkout or ≤3 steps with progress, and inline validation.
- **Commitment ladder:** guest browse → save favourites → quiz → plan reveal → checkout. Each step adds investment.
