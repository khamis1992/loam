# Rendered JavaScript link coverage — revision 34

The browser traversal previously stopped collecting child routes at the depth boundary, silently dropped links when its queue reached 100 entries, and sampled 500 anchors without recording that the sample was truncated. Duplicate links could fill the queue. These conditions could obscure why a short assessment did not visit known pages, even though static HTML discovery already tracked its own limits.

`BrowserFrontier` now tracks distinct known URLs from rendered anchors. Browser results retain `discovery.limits` for depth, page, queue and interrupted traversal, `linkLimitedPages` for documents with more than 500 anchors, and at most 100 sanitized unvisited-URL samples. A later visit removes a deferred URL; an already queued seed is not counted twice. Queue entries are deduplicated before applying capacity limits.

Any known omission or truncated anchor collection sets browser and campaign coverage to partial. A `browser_discovery_coverage` error finding propagates to JSON, Markdown and SARIF, preventing a successful SARIF invocation from concealing this coverage gap. Diagnostics explain the counts, and the assets/coverage UI has a separate JavaScript-link section. Reports from older revisions remain readable without this optional field.

Counts concern known unvisited URLs, not the total number of undiscovered routes. Traversal still excludes query and fragment links, observes only anchors after the configured readiness wait, and does not systematically explore arbitrary buttons, state-dependent navigation, shadow roots or every SPA state. Explicit flows and seeds remain necessary. Complete observed traversal is not comprehensive application coverage. A page attempted but failing readiness remains a failed render proof rather than an unvisited URL.
